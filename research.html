Research

Connecting atoms, networks, and data to material performance.

My research program combines atomistic modeling, machine learning, and materials informatics across polymer networks and amorphous systems.

Research thrust 01

Polymer networks and dental biomaterials

Crosslinked dental resins must balance conversion, mechanical stiffness, dimensional stability, free volume, and processing behavior. I use molecular simulation to connect monomer structure and curing history to the properties of the resulting network.

Virtual curingDegree of conversion Elastic modulusVolumetric shrinkage Fractional free volumeGlass transition

Research thrust 02

Materials informatics and AI-guided discovery

Large molecular design spaces cannot be explored exhaustively with expensive simulations. I develop data-efficient workflows that combine molecular descriptors, surrogate models, uncertainty, active learning, and multi-objective Bayesian optimization.

A central requirement is chemical realism: generated or screened candidates should satisfy structural filters and remain connected to explicit reaction strategies.

Gaussian-process surrogatesActive learning Multi-objective optimizationPareto analysis Molecular descriptorsReaction-aware design

Research thrust 03

Amorphous systems and machine-learning potentials

Amorphous materials are challenging because their properties depend on collective organization beyond the nearest-neighbor environment. I use large-scale machine-learning molecular dynamics and diffraction-based validation to study liquid-to-glass evolution and medium-range order.

Neutron diffractionHigh-energy X-ray diffraction Pair distribution functionsStructure factors Ring statisticsPersistent homology

Shared workflow

How the research directions connect

Across polymers and glasses, the same logic applies: build realistic structures, quantify uncertainty, evaluate physically meaningful descriptors, and connect computation to experiment or application.

1Represent

Encode chemistry, structure, and processing conditions.

2Simulate

Generate atomistic or molecular evidence at relevant conditions.

3Validate

Compare with experiment, literature, uncertainty, and physical constraints.
