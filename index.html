Atoms · Networks · Applications

Designing materials with simulation, physics, and AI.

I am a PhD student in Computational Materials Science at the University at Buffalo, working with Dr. Ganesh Sivaraman and the GS Lab . My research lies at the intersection of molecular dynamics, machine-learning interatomic potentials, and materials informatics, with a focus on understanding how molecular and network structure controls experimentally meaningful properties.

I am especially interested in polymer networks for dental and biomedical applications, the data-driven discovery of chemically realistic materials, and short- to medium-range order in amorphous systems.

Research interests

Connecting molecular structure to real-world material performance

My work combines physical modeling with modern machine learning. The objective is not only to predict properties, but also to explain them, identify realistic design opportunities, and support experimental decision-making.

01

Polymer networks and dental resins

Virtual curing, thermomechanical property prediction, and multi-objective molecular design for safer and higher-performing crosslinked polymer systems.

  • Degree of conversion and network formation
  • Elastic modulus, shrinkage, free volume, and glass transition
  • BPA-free and reaction-aware molecular design
02

Amorphous networks and ML potentials

Large-scale molecular dynamics and diffraction-informed analysis of disordered materials, with emphasis on the limits of machine-learning interatomic potentials.

  • Short- and medium-range network order
  • Neutron and high-energy X-ray diffraction comparison
  • Liquid-to-glass structure and sampling

Highlighted research

Selected projects

These projects illustrate my broader approach: use computation to discover structure-property relationships, test them against physical evidence, and translate the results into useful materials design decisions.

Active learning Molecular dynamics Dental materials

AI-guided discovery of BPA-free dental resin backbones

A multi-objective active-learning workflow for navigating chemically filtered molecular libraries and prioritizing candidates for expensive virtual-curing simulations. The optimization balances elastic modulus, volumetric shrinkage, fractional free volume, and network conversion.

Application: safer, lower-shrinkage, and mechanically reliable dental restorative materials.

ML potentials Neutron and X-Ray Diffraction Glassy Silica

Limits of long-range machine learning interatomic potentials in glassy silica

Large-scale molecular dynamics is compared directly with neutron and high-energy X-ray diffraction to assess whether short-range and long-range machine-learning models reproduce liquid and glass structure beyond the local tetrahedral environment.

Application: reliable predictive models for network glasses used in optics, electronics, and advanced manufacturing.
