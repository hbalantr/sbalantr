Atoms · Networks · Applications

Designing materials with simulations, physics, and AI.

I am a PhD student in the materials design and innovation department at the University at Buffalo, working with Dr. Ganesh Sivaraman . My research lies at the intersection of molecular dynamics, machine-learning interatomic potentials, DFT, and materials informatics, with a focus on understanding how molecular and network structure controls experimentally meaningful properties.

I am especially interested in amorphous polymer networks for dental and biomedical applications, the data-driven discovery of chemically realistic materials, and short-to medium-range order in amorphous systems.

About me

Computational research grounded in physical materials.

I combine molecular simulation, density functional theory (DFT), machine learning, and materials informatics to study how chemistry, network architecture, processing history, and collective structure determine material properties.

My current research spans crosslinked polymer networks for dental and biomedical materials, multi-objective discovery of chemically realistic molecular candidates, and experiment-grounded modeling of amorphous silica.

I value physically interpretable models, uncertainty-aware exploration, reproducible computational workflows, and direct comparison with experimentally meaningful measurements.

Research approach

From molecular structure to material decisions.

I treat computation as a connected workflow rather than an isolated prediction. Models should be physically grounded, validated against meaningful measurements, and used to guide the next simulation or experiment.

1 Model

Build chemically and physically realistic molecular or atomistic systems.

2 Learn

Use simulation, machine learning, and uncertainty to reveal structure–property relationships.

3 Translate

Connect predictions to dental materials, polymer performance, and experimental structure.
